# Scientific synthesis skill

Write an argument, not a pile of paper summaries.

## Before drafting

1. State one thesis sentence that answers the user's focus.
2. Group the material into 3–5 themes. A theme is a claim, not a paper.
3. For each theme, select the experiment cards that support or challenge it.
4. Put the strongest counter-evidence inside the theme it qualifies; do not hide it in a limitations appendix.

## Paragraph contract

Each paragraph must do exactly one of these jobs:

- make one claim;
- present the evidence for that claim;
- interpret or qualify that claim.

Start with the topic sentence. Then the evidence. Then the interpretation. No paragraph should be a tour of several papers.

## Use of experiment records

- Experiment cards are primary evidence, not decoration.
- When an experiment card has a value, unit and context, use them together.
- If the paper reports no value, say `not reported`; do not replace it with a qualitative guess.
- Never merge results from different experimental conditions into one number.

## Structure

- One thesis.
- 3–5 themes.
- Discussion organised by theme, not by paper.
- One short limitations section that names the strongest threat to the thesis.
- Conclusion answers the focus directly; no new claims.

## Prohibited

- "Paper [n] proposes..." as the main sentence of every paragraph.
- Listing all sources one by one.
- Restating the abstract as a section.
- Generic filler such as "this is important" without a specific reason.
- Claims stronger than the cited evidence.
